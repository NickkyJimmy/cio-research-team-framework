## Objective Research Guide

Use this guide to transform vague requests into objective-led research plans.

## 1) Objective Cascade

Translate in this order:

1. Business metric (north star or KPI)
2. Decision that stakeholders need to make
3. Research objective
4. Sub-objectives (2-5 concrete questions)
5. Evidence requirements (what data is needed)

If any step is missing, ask for clarification or propose a best-guess and label assumptions.

## 2) Objective Quality Rubric

A strong research objective contains all three:

- Measurable or observable outcome
- Explicit user segment
- Explicit decision linkage

Example:

- Weak: "Understand user behavior"
- Strong: "Identify the top three barriers reducing first-transfer completion among new wallet users in week 1, to prioritize onboarding fixes for Q3 release"

## 3) Objective Type -> Research Intent

Use these intent tags to choose methods later.

- Measure prevalence/lift/comparison -> Quantitative intent
- Explain motivations/mental models/journey friction -> Qualitative intent
- Need both scale and mechanism -> Mixed intent

## 4) Decision Framing Prompts

Use these prompts when objective quality is weak:

- "After this research, what decision will change?"
- "What metric should move if this works?"
- "Which user segment matters most for this decision?"
- "What would count as success vs failure?"
- "Is the budget proportional to the decision at stake?"

## 5) Objective Anti-patterns

Flag and rewrite these cases:

- "Research everything" (scope too broad)
- No target segment
- No timeline or decision owner
- Method-first requests with no objective fit (for example "do FGD" without why)
- Budget not considered relative to decision importance

## 6) Output Block Template

### Objective Quality Check
- Business metric: [name + baseline + target]
- Decision to inform: [decision + owner + date]
- Research objective: [single sentence]
- Sub-objectives:
  1. ...
  2. ...
  3. ...
- Coverage status: [pass / needs clarification]
