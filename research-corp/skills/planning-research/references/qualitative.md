## Qualitative Research Reference

Use qualitative methods when objectives ask "why" and "how": motivations, mental models, journey friction, concept exploration, language, and behavioral context.

## 1) Method Selection Matrix

Choose method by decision need, not preference.

- IDI (in-depth interview):
  - Best for personal experience, sensitive topics, financial behavior, or context-heavy journeys.
  - Use when depth of individual mechanism matters more than group interaction.
- FGD (focus group discussion):
  - Best for social language, concept reactions, message framing, and idea generation.
  - Use when interaction effects are part of the objective.
- In-home / contextual immersion:
  - Best for real-world behavior, environmental constraints, and unmet workflow needs.
  - Use when claimed behavior and observed behavior are likely to differ.
- Online diary / mobile ethnography:
  - Best for longitudinal behavioral capture over days or weeks.
  - Use when the objective requires understanding behavior in natural context over time, not just recalled in an interview.

Guardrails:

- Do not use qual to claim prevalence or market share.
- Do not choose FGD for highly sensitive topics where social desirability bias is high.
- If objective is both mechanism and scale, propose mixed design (qual first, quant follow-up).

## 2) Sample Design and Recruitment

### Typical sizing

- IDI: 8-15 interviews per key segment.
- FGD: 2-3 groups per segment, 6-8 participants per group.
- Immersion: small but high-context sample, selected for behavioral contrast.
- Online diary: 15-30 participants per segment, 5-14 day diary period.

Increase sample only when objective requires additional behavioral strata.

### Segment definition rules

- Define segments behaviorally first, demographics second.
- Include at least one contrast segment when decision risk is medium/high.
- Avoid fragmented micro-segments that dilute insight quality.

### Recruitment checklist

- Clear inclusion/exclusion criteria linked to objective.
- Recruitment feasibility rating per segment (high/medium/low).
- Backup recruit list for no-show risk.
- Incentive and scheduling plan suitable for target participants.

## 3) Strengths and Limits

- Strength:
  - Explains mechanism, context, and unmet needs.
  - Useful for hypothesis generation and solution framing.
- Limit:
  - Cannot estimate prevalence or lift with statistical confidence.
  - Findings require transparent caveats when generalized.

## 4) Discussion Guide Design Standards

Map every section to sub-objective and decision use.

Recommended flow:

1. Context warm-up (current behavior and routines).
2. Journey reconstruction (recent real episode).
3. Trigger and barrier deep-dive (why action happened or failed).
4. Concept or stimulus probes (if testing solutions/messages).
5. Decision probes (what must change to shift behavior).

Question-writing rules:

- Start open, then narrow.
- Use neutral probes ("tell me more", "what made that hard").
- Avoid leading or confirmatory phrasing.
- Include contradiction probes ("you said X earlier, help me understand Y").

## 5) Moderation Quality Standards

### IDI standards

- Build rapport quickly and protect psychological safety.
- Probe behavior and concrete episodes before opinions.
- Distinguish stated preference vs actual behavior.

### FGD standards

- Control dominant participants and invite quieter voices.
- Separate consensus language from individual conviction.
- Capture group dynamics as data, not noise.

### Bias controls

- Avoid moderator approval cues.
- Rotate stimulus order when possible.
- Keep note-taking structure consistent across sessions.

## 6) Analysis Workflow

Use a transparent evidence pipeline.

1. Debrief after each session (quick memo + emerging signals).
2. Build initial coding schema from objectives and emergent themes.
3. Code transcripts/notes consistently across segments.
4. Synthesize themes with supporting verbatims.
5. Map each theme to decision implications and confidence level.

### Theme quality criteria

- Repeated across multiple participants or contexts.
- Mechanism is explainable (not only descriptive).
- Supported by direct evidence (quotes, observed behavior).
- Linked to an actionable decision.

Handle contradictions explicitly:

- Keep both patterns if they reflect real segment differences.
- Do not flatten opposing evidence into one vague theme.

## 7) Deliverable Structure for Decision Use

Each major finding should include all four parts:

- Theme: what pattern is happening.
- Evidence: verbatim or observed example.
- Implication: why it matters for business objective.
- Decision use: what action the stakeholder can take.

Recommended insight block template:

Theme: [short statement]
- Evidence: "[verbatim quote]"
- Mechanism: [why this happens]
- Segment scope: [where this appears]
- Decision use: [what to change or prioritize]

## 8) Common Misuse to Avoid

- Using FGD/IDI findings to make numeric population claims.
- Over-generalizing from small n without caveats.
- Running qual without clear decision hypotheses.
- Reporting only quotes without mechanism or implication.

## 9) Online and Digital Qualitative Methods

When face-to-face is not feasible or when the objective benefits from digital approaches.

### Remote IDI (video call)
- Platform: Zoom, Teams, Google Meet (with recording consent)
- Best for: geographically dispersed participants, sensitive topics where home comfort matters
- Limitations: harder to read body language, technology barriers for some segments
- Tips: send tech check instructions beforehand, keep sessions 45-60 min max

### Online Community / Bulletin Board
- Platform: dedicated qual platforms (Recollective, Remesh, etc.) or private social groups
- Best for: multi-day exploration, co-creation tasks, diary-like reflection
- Sample: 20-40 participants per community, 3-7 day duration
- Limitations: requires active moderation, self-selection bias, lower engagement over time
- Tips: mix task types (photo upload, short write, poll, discussion), daily moderation

### Mobile Ethnography
- Platform: dedicated apps (dscout, Indeemo, EthOS) or simple WhatsApp/Zalo diary
- Best for: capturing real-time behavior in natural context, shopper journeys, usage occasions
- Sample: 15-30 participants, 5-14 day capture period
- Limitations: participant fatigue, data volume management, privacy sensitivity
- Tips: clear daily prompts, keep tasks under 10 minutes, use multimedia (photo + voice + text)

### Hybrid Approaches
- Use online methods for exploration/screening, then follow up with select in-person IDIs for depth.
- Online methods work well as a pre-task before FGD sessions (participants arrive primed).

## 10) Output Quality Checklist

Before finalizing a qual section in the plan, verify:

- Every sub-objective has at least one qual evidence path.
- Discussion guide sections map to objectives.
- Segment logic is behavioral and decision-relevant.
- Analysis method (coding/thematic synthesis) is explicit.
- Each insight includes evidence + implication + decision use.
- Limits and transferability caveats are clearly stated.
- If using online methods, technology requirements and feasibility are noted.
